1. Topic Overview

Definition

The 7 Patterns of AI are a classification framework stating that every AI use case, however complex, maps to one or more of seven repeatable patterns. They are pattern-shapes of business problems, independent of specific algorithms or technologies.

One-line memory hook: If a use case maps to no pattern, it's probably not an AI problem.

Why It Is Important

  • Used in Phase I to validate that a problem genuinely needs AI.
  • Helps estimate data needs, complexity, and risk (each pattern has typical requirements).
  • Provides a common language between business and technical teams.
  • Combinations of patterns describe even the most advanced systems (a self-driving car = several patterns together).

Where It Is Used in AI Projects

  • Phase I: map problem → pattern(s) → go/no-go.
  • Phase II: each pattern implies typical data types (images for Recognition, interaction logs for Hyperpersonalization).
  • Phase IV: patterns suggest algorithm families.
  • Portfolio planning: classify all enterprise AI ideas by pattern.

2. Key Concepts — The Seven Patterns Explained

Pattern 1: Hyperpersonalization

  • Idea: Treat each individual as a unique segment of one — learn preferences and tailor content, offers, pricing, or experiences.
  • Core question: "What does THIS person want?"
  • Data: behavior history, clicks, purchases, profiles.
  • Examples: Netflix/Amazon recommendations; personalized banking offers; adaptive e-learning; personalized medicine dosing.
  • Keywords: recommendation, personalized, individual, tailored, segment-of-one.

Pattern 2: Autonomous Systems

  • Idea: Systems (physical or software) that accomplish tasks with minimal or no human involvement.
  • Core question: "Can the machine act on its own?"
  • Data/tech: sensors, environment models, control loops; often combines other patterns.
  • Examples: self-driving vehicles; warehouse robots; autonomous drones; auto-scaling software agents; autonomous trading bots.
  • Keywords: self-driving, autonomous, minimal human intervention, robot acting independently.

Pattern 3: Predictive Analytics & Decision Support

  • Idea: Use past data to predict future outcomes or values and help humans decide better.
  • Core question: "What will happen next?" (human still decides)
  • Data: historical structured data with outcomes.
  • Examples: loan default prediction; demand forecasting; churn prediction; predictive maintenance; disease-risk scoring.
  • Keywords: forecast, predict, likelihood, risk score, decision support, what will happen.

Pattern 4: Conversation & Human Interaction

  • Idea: Machines communicating with humans in natural, human ways — text, voice — and understanding intent, sentiment, language.
  • Core question: "Can the machine talk/understand like a human?"
  • Data: text corpora, dialogs, speech audio.
  • Examples: chatbots, voice assistants (Alexa/Siri), sentiment analysis, machine translation, content generation.
  • Keywords: chatbot, voice assistant, NLP, dialogue, sentiment, translate.

Pattern 5: Patterns & Anomalies

  • Idea: Find hidden patterns/associations in data, or detect the outlier that doesn't fit the pattern.
  • Core question: "What's normal — and what's NOT normal?"
  • Data: large transactional/log datasets; often unlabeled.
  • Examples: fraud detection; network intrusion detection; fake-news/bot detection; predictive quality (spot defective sensor readings); market-basket association ("bread buyers buy butter").
  • Keywords: fraud, anomaly, outlier, unusual, intrusion, hidden pattern, association.

Pattern 6: Recognition

  • Idea: Identify and label things in unstructured data — images, video, audio, text, handwriting.
  • Core question: "What is this thing?"
  • Data: labeled unstructured data (images with tags, transcribed audio).
  • Examples: face recognition; tumor detection in X-rays; OCR of cheques/invoices; speech-to-text; defect detection on production lines; object detection for security cameras.
  • Keywords: image, vision, OCR, detect object, classify picture, speech-to-text, identify.

Pattern 7: Goal-Driven Systems

  • Idea: Systems that learn through trial and error to find the optimal solution to a goal — typically reinforcement learning.
  • Core question: "What's the best move to reach the goal?"
  • Data/tech: environment + reward signals (often simulation).
  • Examples: game-playing AI (AlphaGo); route/logistics optimization; dynamic resource allocation; bidding optimization; robot motion learning.
  • Keywords: optimize, trial-and-error, reward, best strategy, game, win, scenario simulation.

Combining Patterns

Real systems stack patterns:

System Patterns Combined
Self-driving car Recognition (see objects) + Predictive (anticipate pedestrians) + Goal-Driven (route) + Autonomous (acts alone)
Smart voice assistant Conversation + Hyperpersonalization + Predictive
Fraud platform Patterns & Anomalies + Predictive + (Recognition for document checks)

3. PMI CPMAI Exam Focus

What the Exam Expects

  • Map any scenario to its pattern(s) — the single most common question style.
  • Know each pattern's core question, data needs, and 2–3 examples.
  • Understand patterns are technology-independent and can combine.
  • Use patterns for the Phase I "is this AI?" test.

Frequently Tested Mappings

Scenario Cue Pattern
"Recommend products / personalize offers" Hyperpersonalization
"Robot/vehicle operates without humans" Autonomous Systems
"Forecast / predict / risk score" Predictive Analytics
"Chatbot / voice / understand language" Conversation & Human Interaction
"Fraud / intrusion / unusual activity" Patterns & Anomalies
"Identify objects in images/audio/documents" Recognition
"Learn best strategy via rewards / optimize" Goal-Driven Systems

Common Misconceptions

Misconception Truth
"Fraud detection = Recognition" Recognition = unstructured media ID; fraud = Patterns & Anomalies
"Prediction = Goal-Driven" Predictive forecasts outcomes; Goal-Driven learns actions via rewards
"Chatbot = Hyperpersonalization" Talking = Conversation; tailoring content = Hyperpersonalization (may combine)
"Autonomous = any automation" RPA scripts aren't autonomous AI; autonomy involves perception+decision in the world
"One use case = exactly one pattern" Patterns combine

4. AI Project Manager Perspective

Responsibilities

  • Facilitate pattern-mapping workshops in Phase I.
  • Use the pattern to set expectations on data needs, complexity, timeline:
  • Recognition → needs labeled unstructured data (labeling budget!).
  • Goal-Driven → may need simulation environments.
  • Conversation → language data, dialog design, escalation paths.
  • Classify the AI portfolio by pattern for skill and platform planning.
  • Reject/redirect ideas that map to no pattern (probably RPA/rules/BI).

Stakeholders

  • Business owners (use-case framing), data scientists (feasibility per pattern), UX (Conversation pattern), safety/legal (Autonomous & Recognition privacy issues), operations (deployment implications per pattern).

Risks by Pattern (sample)

Pattern Signature Risk
Hyperpersonalization Privacy, filter bubbles, creepiness factor
Autonomous Safety, liability, edge-case failures
Predictive Bias in historical data, drift
Conversation Wrong answers, brand/tone risk, prompt abuse
Patterns & Anomalies False positives flood, adversarial adaptation
Recognition Bias (face recognition), privacy/surveillance concerns
Goal-Driven Reward hacking, unexpected strategies, sim-to-real gap

Best Practices

  • Always document the pattern mapping in the Phase I business case.
  • For combined-pattern systems, start small with one pattern (iterate!).
  • Match team skills to the pattern (CV engineers for Recognition, NLP for Conversation).

Decision-Making Approach

Business use case
   ▼
Which core question does it match?
 "What does this person want?"        → Hyperpersonalization
 "Can it act by itself?"              → Autonomous
 "What will happen?"                  → Predictive
 "Can it converse/understand text?"   → Conversation
 "What's abnormal / hidden pattern?"  → Patterns & Anomalies
 "What is this (image/audio/doc)?"    → Recognition
 "What's the best action to a goal?"  → Goal-Driven
   ▼
No match? → Likely NOT an AI problem (rules/RPA/BI instead)

5. Real-World Example (One Company, All 7 Patterns — Retail Bank)

A bank builds an AI portfolio; the PM maps each idea:

  1. Personalized product offers in the mobile app based on each customer's transactions → Hyperpersonalization.
  2. Loan default risk scores for underwriting decisions → Predictive Analytics & Decision Support.
  3. WhatsApp banking chatbot answering balance/EMI questions in Hindi & English → Conversation & Human Interaction.
  4. Card fraud detection flagging unusual transactions in real time → Patterns & Anomalies.
  5. Cheque OCR reading amounts and signatures from scanned cheques → Recognition.
  6. ATM cash-loading route optimization learning best replenishment strategy via simulated rewards → Goal-Driven Systems.
  7. Autonomous document-processing agents that receive, classify, extract, validate, and post loan documents end-to-end with human review only on exceptions → Autonomous Systems (combining Recognition + Conversation).

Step-by-step PM use: each idea gets a pattern label → pattern implies data/skill needs → prioritized by ROI and feasibility → "start small" pilots chosen (fraud + chatbot first) → others queued for later iterations.

7. Key Formulas or Frameworks

Pattern Mapping Test (Phase I)

1. State the business problem in one sentence.
2. Match to a pattern core question.
3. No match → challenge whether AI is needed.
4. Multiple matches → decompose; start with ONE pattern (start small).

Pattern → Typical Data → Typical ML

Pattern Typical Data Typical ML Family

Autonomous Sensor streams Multi-pattern + control/RL
Predictive Historical tabular Supervised (classif./regression)
Conversation Text/speech NLP / language models
Patterns & Anomalies Transactions/logs Unsupervised + supervised hybrids
Recognition Images/audio/docs Deep learning (CNNs etc.)
Goal-Driven Simulated environments Reinforcement learning

9. Exam Tips

Memory Tricks

  • Mnemonic sentence: "Happy Aunts Prepare Chai, Pakode Roz Garma-garam" → Hyperpersonalization, Autonomous, Predictive, Conversation, Patterns&Anomalies, Recognition, Goal-driven. 🍵
  • Core-question flash: personalize? act alone? predict? talk? odd-one-out? what-is-this? best-move?

Keyword → Pattern Reflexes

  • recommend/tailor → 1 | self-driving/robot → 2 | forecast/risk score → 3
  • chatbot/voice/translate → 4 | fraud/intrusion/unusual → 5
  • image/OCR/speech-to-text → 6 | optimize via reward/game → 7

Confusion Killers

Confusable Resolution
Recognition vs Anomalies Media identification vs behavioral oddity
Predictive vs Goal-Driven Forecast outcome vs learn best action
Conversation vs Hyperpersonalization Language ability vs individual tailoring
Autonomous vs RPA Perceive-decide-act in the world vs scripted clicks

Traps

  • Fraud labeled "Recognition" → wrong (it's Patterns & Anomalies).
  • Assuming a use case can only have one pattern → combinations exist.
  • Treating RPA/rule automation as the Autonomous pattern → wrong.

11. Quick Revision Sheet

  • 7 Patterns = universal classification of AI use cases; no pattern match → probably not AI.
  • 1 Hyperpersonalization — segment of one; recommendations, tailored offers.
  • 2 Autonomous Systems — acts with minimal human help; vehicles, robots, agents.
  • 3 Predictive Analytics — forecast outcomes, decision support; churn, default, demand.
  • 4 Conversation & Human Interaction — chatbots, voice, NLP, sentiment, translation.
  • 5 Patterns & Anomalies — fraud, intrusion, odd-one-out, associations.
  • 6 Recognition — identify things in images/audio/video/docs; OCR, face, tumor.
  • 7 Goal-Driven Systems — reinforcement learning; optimize via rewards.
  • Patterns are technology-independent and combine in real systems.
  • Used in Phase I for AI validation; imply data/skill needs for later phases.
  • Fraud ≠ Recognition — it's Patterns & Anomalies (classic trap).

⭐ Top 10 Things to Remember for the Exam

  1. Every AI use case maps to one or more of the 7 patterns — no match ⇒ not AI.
  2. Hyperpersonalization = segment of one (recommendations, offers).
  3. Autonomous = acts with minimal human involvement (not scripted RPA).
  4. Predictive = forecast + decision support (human decides).
  5. Conversation = NLP/chat/voice/sentiment/translation.
  6. Patterns & Anomalies = fraud, intrusion, odd-one-out ← fraud lives HERE.
  7. Recognition = identify things in unstructured media (OCR, vision, speech-to-text).
  8. Goal-Driven = reinforcement learning, rewards, optimal strategy.
  9. Patterns combine — scope iteration 1 around one pattern (start small).
  10. Patterns are technology-independent problem shapes, mapped in Phase I.
